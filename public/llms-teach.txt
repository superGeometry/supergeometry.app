# Teaching superGeometry.app

For an AI assistant working in the person's tab, when they want to **learn** the app ("teach me", "how do I use this", "show me how to...") and not just have a part made.

## How to teach

- **The person does the tapping.** You say what to tap and why; they do it. Do not build with `sg.make`, `sg.add` or `sg.change` in a lesson. Only if they ask you to show a step, or cannot get past it after two tries, do that one step for them, say what you did, and hand back.
- **First ask two things**, in one message: what they would like to be able to make, and whether they have used a CAD program before. Then pick where to start (see "Where to start").
- **One step at a time.** Give one or two taps, with one sentence of why. Then wait for them. Keep each message short: this is a conversation, not a manual.
- **Look, don't guess.** When they say they have done it, or that it did not work, run `sg.now()`. It answers in a line or two: the tool that is open, the sketch they are in and what is drawn in it with its sizes, what is picked, the last step, each body's size, and `says` (what the app is telling them right now). It costs almost nothing. Do not take screenshots.
- **Words in bold are exactly what is on the screen.** Say them as written. If `sg.now()` shows something else, trust the app: tell them what you see and go from there.
- **Praise what worked, name what happened.** "That made Extrude 01, 20 tall" teaches the vocabulary. If something went wrong, say what the app did and the one tap that undoes it (Undo is bottom left, or Ctrl + Z).
- **Let them wander.** If they try something off the lesson, answer it. Come back to the lesson when they are ready.
- Fetch one lesson at a time with `await sg.teach(3)`, when you start it. Each ends with what `sg.now()` should show, and the slips people make there.

## How the app works, in six lines (tell them this first, in your own words)

1. Everything is: draw a flat shape (a **sketch**), turn it into a solid, then pick parts of the solid and change them.
2. **Pick first.** Tap a face, an edge or a body: the line at the bottom of the screen then shows what can be done with it, by group: **Sketch**, **Freeform**, **Transform**, **Construct**, **Modify**, **Combine**, **Create**, and **More**. Round buttons beside the pick hold the likeliest tools.
3. A tool open turns the line into its settings. Tap a number to type it. The tick finishes; the cross takes it back.
4. Every step is listed in **History** (top bar). Tap a step to open it again and change it; everything after it follows.
5. Turn the view: right-drag with a mouse, one finger on a touch screen. Zoom: wheel, or pinch. Move: middle-drag, or two fingers. Double-tap the view cube (top right) to fit everything. On a touch screen two fingers twisted roll the view.
6. Nothing is lost: Undo and Redo are at the bottom left (on a touch screen also a tap on the view with two fingers, and with three), and the project saves itself in the browser.

## The course builds one thing

Lessons 1 to 9 build a desk lamp in one project, a part a lesson: a round base on three feet, a pole, a flared shade that tilts, a bulb inside it. Each lesson needs the lamp as the one before left it. At the end the tilt, the wall and the number of feet are numbers they can change, and it is ready to print. Tell them this at the start, in a sentence, and now and then how far along they are.

The lessons' sizes fit each other (a base 12 tall and a pole of 200 put the pole's top at 212, which lessons 5 and 7 use). If theirs differ, read them with `sg.now()` and give the numbers that fit their lamp.

## Where to start

- **New to CAD:** lesson 1, and on through the lamp in order. They can stop anywhere: the project saves itself.
- **Has used another CAD program:** say what is the same (sketch, extrude, fillet, shell, a history of steps) and what differs (you pick first and the tools come to the pick; no toolbar of icons; sizes are typed on the line or on the model). Then the lamp, quickly.
- **Wants one specific thing** ("how do I make a hole for an M4 screw"): the lesson that has it, only that part, on a part of their own or a quick block.
- **Has a part in mind:** teach through that part, with their sizes. Lessons 10 to 12 hold what the lamp does not need.
- **Comes back to a lamp half built:** `sg.now()` lists its steps and bodies: go on from the next missing part.

## When they are stuck

- Nothing happens on a tap: a tool is still open (finish it with the tick, or the cross), or they are still in a sketch (**Exit** at the end of the line; with a drawing tool on, the tick first: it puts the tool down, and Exit is there again).
- No round buttons beside a pick: **Quick buttons** is switched off in **Settings** (the gear at the right edge). The same tools are on the line: use those, or have them switch it on.
- The tool they want is not on the line: the line shows what fits the pick. Tap empty space to drop the pick; every tool is then there and asks for its own pick.
- A step shows a red mark in History: `sg.now()` gives `errors` with the reason in words. Read it to them and fix that step.
- They cannot hit a small thing: zoom in, or tap near it. A double tap on the model picks the whole body.

## The lessons

The lamp:

1. The base: a first solid, and looking around it.
2. Changing it: a rounded rim, and going back in History.
3. The pole: drawing on a face.
4. The shade: a second body, flared and hollow.
5. The bulb: a smooth shape with Freeform.
6. Three feet: a hole, and copies of it in a circle.
7. The head tilted: turning two bodies together.
8. Sizes with names: variables.
9. Out to the printer: checking, exporting, saving.

Beside the lamp, for when they ask:

10. Drawing exactly: sizes and constraints, cutting a pocket.
11. Round parts: a knob with Revolve.
12. Two parts that fit: a box and its lid.

## Lesson 1: The base

Makes: a disc 120 across and 12 tall. Teaches: a sketch, pulling it into a solid, looking around.

1. If the welcome window is up, **New project**. A new project opens already in a sketch on the floor, seen from above, with **Line** on. (Any other time: **Sketch** on the line, then **XY (floor)**.)
2. Tap **Draw**, then **Circle**. The line now shows **Center**, **2 Points** and **3 Points**: **Center** should be the lit one; tap it if it is not.
3. Click where the red and the green line cross: that is the middle of everything. Move the pointer out, type `120` and press Enter. The number is the circle's size across (its diameter). (Touch: tap the middle, tap again further out, then tap the circle and type its size on the line.)
4. Tap **Exit** at the end of the line (it pulses). The view swings round and the circle is picked, with an arrow on it.
5. Type `12` and press Enter. That pulls the circle up into a solid 12 tall and finishes the step. (Or drag the arrow up and let go.)
6. Now look around: turn, zoom and move the view (line 5 of "How the app works"). Tap a face of the view cube, top right, for a straight-on view. Double-tap the cube to fit everything.

Check with `sg.now()`: `steps` 2; `last` is "Extrude 01: extrude 12"; one body of `size` [120, 120, 12].
Likely slips: drawing with **Line** because **Circle** was not chosen (Undo, then **Draw**, **Circle**); a circle of two taps because **2 Points** was lit; forgetting **Exit**, so taps keep drawing.
Say once: a typed size and Enter finishes most steps. Dragging does the same by eye.

## Lesson 2: Changing it

Needs: the base. Teaches: that a part is changed by picking it; **Fillet**; **History**; Undo.

1. Tap the top rim of the base, the round edge. It lights up, and round buttons show beside it: **Fillet** and **Chamfer** first.
2. Tap **Fillet**. The rim is rounded at once, by a size the app chose from the part. Type `4` and press Enter (or drag the dot), then the tick if the tool is still open.
3. Open **History** (top bar). It lists Sketch 01, Extrude 01, Fillet 01: every step, in order. Tap **Extrude 01**: its tool opens again with the old value. Change **Distance** to `20` and tap the tick. The base is taller and the rounded rim came with it. This is what "parametric" means.
4. Tap Undo (bottom left, or Ctrl + Z): the base is 12 tall again. Redo is beside it. Leave it at 12.
5. In History, the eye beside a step switches it off without deleting it; the bin deletes it. Close History.

Check: `last` is "Fillet 01: fillet 4 on 1 edge(s)"; the body's third size is 12 again after step 4.
Likely slips: tapping the flat top when the rim was meant (tap again on the edge itself, or zoom in); leaving the Fillet tool open and wondering why other taps add edges (the tick first).

## Lesson 3: The pole

Needs: the base. Teaches: a sketch on a face; that a shape pulled out of a face joins the body.

1. Tap the flat top of the base (not the rim). Round buttons show: tap **Sketch**. The view turns to face the top, and you are drawing on it.
2. **Draw**, **Circle**. Click the middle of the top: the pointer catches on it. Type `12`, Enter.
3. **Exit**. The small circle is picked, with its arrow.
4. Type `200`, Enter. A pole 200 tall. On a face, out adds to the body and in cuts into it: so the base and the pole are one body.
5. Double-tap the view cube to see all of it.

Check: `last` is "Extrude 02: extrude 200"; still one body, now of `size` [120, 120, 212].
Likely slips: the circle off the middle (Undo, and click nearer the middle; a small mark shows when it has caught); a typed minus sign, which cuts a hole down into the base instead (Undo).

## Lesson 4: The shade

Needs: the pole. Teaches: a tool's settings on the line; **Operation** and a second body; **Draft angle**; **Shell**; **Items**.

1. Zoom in on the top of the pole and tap its small flat top. **Sketch**.
2. **Draw**, **Circle**, click the middle, type `32`, Enter. The circle is wider than the pole. **Exit**.
3. This time do not type a height: tap **Extrude**, the round button beside the shape. The line now holds the tool's settings. (On a phone held upright some are behind **Options**.)
4. Tap **All shapes**. The pole's own outline cuts the circle into a ring and a middle; this takes both.
5. Tap **Distance**, type `70`, Enter. Tap **Draft angle**, type `-20`, Enter: the sides lean out as they rise. (A plus angle leans them in.)
6. Tap **Operation** and choose **New body**. Without this the shade would be added to the pole; as a body of its own it can be hollowed, coloured, turned and printed by itself. Tap the tick.
7. Hollow it. Tap the shade's wide flat top. On the line: **Modify**, then **Shell**. Type the wall, `2`, Enter, and tap the tick. The face tapped is the open one.
8. Open **Items** (top bar): it lists Body 1 (base and pole) and Body 2 (the shade). **Rename** is beside each, and so is **Hide**.

Check: `last` is "Shell 01: shell, walls 2, 1 face(s) open"; two bodies, the second about [83, 83, 70].
Likely slips: the shade joined to the pole, so there is still one body (History, **Extrude 03**, **Operation**, **New body**); only a ring pulled up, with a hole down its middle (**All shapes** was missed: History, Extrude 03, tap it); the sides leaning in (the minus sign).

## Lesson 5: The bulb

Needs: the shade. Teaches: **Freeform**, a shape pulled by its points, for what is not made of flat sketches; hiding a body; **Color**.

1. The bulb goes inside the shade, so put the shade out of the way first: **Items**, and **Hide** beside Body 2.
2. Tap empty space so nothing is picked. On the line: **Freeform**, then **Sphere**. A ball 40 across appears at the middle of the floor, inside the base, with a cage of points round it. All of it is picked, and the line shows **Diameter**, **Move**, **ΔX**, **ΔY**, **ΔZ**.
3. Lift it over the pole: tap **ΔZ**, type `225`, Enter. (The pole's top is at 212.)
4. Tap empty space to let go of the points.
5. Turn the view to look at the ball a little from below. Tap the lowest point of its cage, the one at the very bottom.
6. Tap **ΔZ**, type `-25`, Enter. The ball draws down to a neck that sits on the pole: a bulb. Pulling one point moves the smooth shape near it; that is all Freeform is.
7. Tap the tick.
8. **Items**: show Body 2 again (the same button beside it). The bulb sits inside the shade.
9. Give it a colour: in **Items** tap Body 3, then **More** at the end of the line, **Color**, **Yellow**.

Check: `last` is "Freeform 01: freeform"; three bodies, the third about [40, 40, 51] and near 214 to 265 high.
Likely slips: a point at the side taken, not the bottom one (tap empty space, turn the view lower, tap again); an edge or a face picked (the same); the whole ball moved by -25 because nothing was let go (Undo, tap empty space first).
If they enjoy this: more points picked together (each tap adds, or drag a box round them) move together, **Scale** in place of **Move** makes a waist, and **Add detail** gives more points.

## Lesson 6: Three feet

Needs: the base. Teaches: looking from below; **Hole**; **Pattern – Circular**.

1. Turn the view until you see the underside of the base. Tap it about two thirds of the way out from the middle.
2. On the line: **Create**, then **Hole**. It starts as an M3 screw hole right through, where you tapped.
3. Make it a shallow recess for a stick-on foot: tap **Diameter**, type `12`, Enter. Tap **Through** to switch it off; **Depth** appears: type `1.5`, Enter. Tap the tick.
4. Now two more, evenly round the base. Tap the flat bottom of the recess. On the line: **Transform**, then **Pattern – Circular** (it says "repeats this hole").
5. The line asks for an **Axis**: tap **Z**. Set **Count** to `3`. Tap the tick.
6. Turn the view back up.

Check: `last` is "Hole Pattern 01: circular, 3 in all"; still three bodies.
Likely slips: `bodies` jumped to five: the pattern copied the whole base, because the underside was picked and not the recess (Undo, tap inside the recess, again); a hole right through the base (History, **Hole 01**, **Through** off).
Say once: holes for real screws are the same tool: **Screw** M2 to M12, **Head** for a counterbore or countersink; printed holes come out a little tight, 0.2 to 0.3 mm more on **Diameter** is common.

## Lesson 7: The head tilted

Needs: the shade and the bulb. Teaches: picking more than one thing; **Rotate Around Axis**.

1. The shade and the bulb turn together. Open **Items** and tap Body 2, then Body 3: each tap adds to what is picked. (On the model a double tap picks a whole body.)
2. On the line: **Transform**, then **Rotate Around Axis**.
3. The line asks for an **Axis**: tap **Y**. That is the axis that runs away from you, so the head tips sideways towards X.
4. It has to turn about the top of the pole, not about the floor: tap **Pivot Z**, type `212`, Enter.
5. Tap **Angle**, type `30`, Enter. Tap the tick.

Check: `last` is "Rotate 01: move"; the second body's box now reaches further in X than in Y.
Likely slips: only the shade picked, so the bulb stays behind (Undo, pick both); the head swung far off to the side (the pivot is still at the floor: History, **Rotate 01**, **Pivot Z**).

## Lesson 8: Sizes with names

Needs: the lamp with its tilt, shell and feet. Teaches: **Variables**; a formula in place of a number.

1. Tap empty space. **More**, then **Variables**. **New Variable** opens a small window: Name `tilt`, Value `30`, then **Add Another**. The same for `wall` = `2` and `feet` = `3`. **OK**.
2. Put them to work. **History**, tap **Rotate 01**. Tap **Rotate Y**, and in place of the number type `tilt`, Enter. The line now shows the name with its result. Tap the tick.
3. **History**, **Shell 01**: **Thickness** becomes `wall`. **History**, **Hole Pattern 01**: **Count** becomes `feet`.
4. Now change the lamp from one place: **More**, **Variables**, and give `tilt` the value `50`. The head moves. Try `feet` at `5` and `wall` at `3`.
5. A size can be a sum: anywhere a number goes, `wall * 2` or `tilt + 10` goes too.

Check: `sg.now()` after step 4 shows the second body's box changed; the first body has more faces with five feet.
Likely slips: a name typed that does not exist (the app says so: make the variable first, or mind the spelling); a variable typed into the wrong step (Undo).
If they ask why the pole's height was not made a variable: a sketch on a face follows the face, so the shade would go up with a taller pole. But the bulb was placed, and the head's pivot was typed, as plain numbers, so those two would stay behind. A formula mends the pivot (**Pivot Z**: `12 + poleH`); the bulb would have to be moved up by hand. Knowing which things follow and which were typed is most of what parametric modelling is.

## Lesson 9: Out to the printer

Needs: the lamp.

1. Tap empty space. **More**, **Print / Share**: it says whether the model is fit to print ("Ready to print: all 3 bodies are closed and solid") and its size, and saves it as 3MF, STL or STEP. 3MF keeps sizes and colours; STEP is for other CAD programs.
2. A lamp prints in parts. With a body picked, only that body is exported: pick the shade (a double tap), then **File** (top bar), **Export 3MF**. The same for the bulb and for the base with its pole.
3. For printing the shade lying flat, set `tilt` to `0` first; set it back after.
4. **File**, **Save project** downloads a `.super` file: the whole model with its history, to keep as a backup or open on another device with **File**, **Open…**.
5. The project also saves itself in this browser. Clearing the browser's data for this site deletes it, so the saved file is the safe copy.
6. **File**, **Import file…** brings a STEP, STL, OBJ, 3MF, glTF, FreeCAD, BRep, IFC, DXF or SVG file into the project that is open: a part from somewhere else to build round.

Check: nothing to check in the app; ask them whether the file opened in their slicer.
Then tell them what they now know: a sketch, a solid from it, changing a step in History, drawing on a face, a second body, a hollow one, a freeform shape, holes and patterns, turning bodies, variables. What is left is in lessons 10 to 12, for when they need it, and **More**, **Help** has every tool.

## Lesson 10: Drawing exactly

Needs: a block. Teaches: a sketch on a face, exact sizes, constraints, and cutting.

A block to work on, if there is none: a new project, **Draw**, **Rectangle**, two corners about 40 by 30 apart, **Exit**, type `20`, Enter.

1. Tap the top face, then **Sketch** (the round button beside it, or on the line). The view turns to face it.
2. **Draw**, **Circle**: click a centre on the face, move out, type `8` and Enter.
3. **Draw**, **Line**: draw a few lines; each click is a point, and clicking the first point again closes the shape and ends the line. To stop early, tap the tick: it ends the line and puts the tool down.
4. Tap one of the lines. The line now shows its size on the line at the bottom: tap **Length**, type a number, Enter. That size is now a dimension: it is drawn on the sketch with arrows, and it holds. Click a dimension on the drawing to change it.
5. Pick two lines (Shift + click the second), then **Constraints** on the line: its list has the ones that fit, such as Parallel, Perpendicular and Equal. Tap one. A small mark appears on both lines; point at a mark to see what it ties together.
6. **Exit**. Tap inside the circle: it turns blue with an arrow. Drag the arrow down into the block, or type `-10`: on a face, in cuts and out adds. Tap the tick.

Check: while they draw, `inSketch` is the sketch's name and `drawn` lists their shapes with sizes; `sizes` counts the dimensions and constraints. After step 6 `last` is an extrude with a negative distance (or "(cut)") and the body has more faces.
Likely slips: a typed size "refused": it contradicts a constraint already there; delete that one (pick the line, **Constraints**) or Undo. A shape that will not turn blue is not closed: zoom in on its corners.

## Lesson 11: Round parts

Makes: a knob. Teaches: a sketch on a standing plane, **Revolve**.

1. Tap empty space so nothing is picked. **Sketch**, then the **XZ** button (the front plane).
2. With **Line**, draw half of the knob's outline, to the right of the upright axis: start on the axis, go out along the bottom, up the side, back in to the axis, and down the axis to where you began. Closing the shape ends the line.
3. **Exit**. The shape is picked. Tap **Revolve** (the round button beside it, or **Create**, **Revolve**).
4. The line asks for **Axis**: tap the upright line that lies on the axis (or the **Z** word). **Angle** is 360 for a full turn. Tap the tick.
5. Finish it: tap the top rim, **Chamfer** or **Fillet**.

Check: `last` is "Revolve 01: revolve 360 degrees"; the body is as wide as twice their outline's reach.
Likely slips: an outline that crosses the axis cannot be revolved (keep it on one side); an outline that is not closed has nothing to pick.

## Lesson 12: Two parts that fit

Makes: a box and a lid. Teaches: more than one body, **Operation**, **Combine**.

1. Make a block (as in lesson 10) and hollow it: tap its top, **Modify**, **Shell**, a wall of `2`, the tick.
2. The lid: tap empty space, **Sketch**, tap the box's top rim face. **Draw**, **Rectangle** over the whole box. **Exit**, pull the shape up `3`.
3. Before the tick, look at **Operation** on the line: on a face, out would **Add** to the box. Set it to **New body**, so the lid is its own part. Tap the tick. **Items** (top bar) now lists two bodies; the eye beside one hides it.
4. A lip that fits inside: sketch on the lid's underside a rectangle a little smaller than the opening (0.2 mm clear on each side is a good start for printing), pull it down `3` with **Operation** set to **Add**.
5. **Combine** holds **Union**, **Subtract** and **Intersect** for when two bodies should become one, or one should cut the other: Subtract asks for the body to change, then the bodies to cut with.

Check: two entries in `bodies`; hide the lid and the box is still hollow.
Likely slips: the lid joined to the box (open that Extrude in History and set **Operation** to **New body**).
